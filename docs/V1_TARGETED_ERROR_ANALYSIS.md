## 4. The dominant failure: `make_declaration` recall

**6 of 11 declarations were missed** — 4 became `log_catch`, 2 became `other`.
**Zero declarations were over-predicted** (precision 1.000).

This is a one-directional error. The model has learned a narrow, high-confidence notion of
"declaration" and treats everything outside it as logging. The two intents are genuinely
adjacent in Morisyen — *"anrezistre sa lapes la"* (record this catch) and *"ranz mo rapor"*
(prepare my report) both describe putting catches into the system — and v1 had only **25
declaration records** against 49 logging records.

Because precision is perfect, the fix is **more and broader declaration coverage**, not
disambiguation pressure against false positives.

### `prepare` versus `submit`

Of the 11 declaration records, both `prepare_catch_declaration` and
`submit_mock_declaration` appear. The confusion is at the *intent* layer first — a record
misrouted to `log_catch` never reaches the prepare/submit decision at all. v2 must
therefore fix intent recall before the prepare/submit split can even be measured properly.

## 5. Tool and argument errors

14 tool errors out of 34 (tool accuracy 58.8%):

| Error kind | Count |
|---|---|
| Wrong tool | 8 |
| Missing tool (expected a tool, produced none) | 4 |
| Invented tool (expected none, produced one) | 2 |

**5 of the 14 had the correct intent but the wrong tool.** That is the cleanest signal that
the tool/argument layer is undertrained independently of intent: the model knows *what the
fisher wants* and still picks the wrong function.

Rare tools were badly under-covered in v1 — `queue_for_offline_sync` and
`get_current_demo_date` had **2 records each**.

## 6. Ambiguous Morisyen and mixed-language

`mixed_language_accuracy` and `english_control_accuracy` are `null` in the v1 metrics:
the 34-record internal test split simply contains no mixed-language and no English-control
records. **This is a measurement gap, not a clean result** — v1 cannot tell us whether
mixed-language routing works. v2's challenge set fixes this by including mixed-language
declaration requests explicitly.

`uncertainty_accuracy` was 66.7% — the weakest safety-adjacent behaviour, though no unsafe
output occurred.

## 7. Annotation concerns (recorded, not acted on)

The `other` intent has **support 2** on the internal test with precision 0.500. Any
per-intent number computed on 2 records is noise. Similarly `reviewed_subset_accuracy`
(0.500) was computed over a handful of records and should not be read as a quality signal.

None of these observations were used to change a label. They are reasons to distrust small
per-intent numbers, and are why the v2 gate specifies a floor on *critical* intents rather
than on every intent.

## 8. What v2 must add

Directly derived from the above:

| Finding | v2 response |
|---|---|
| `make_declaration` recall 0.455, 4 leaking to `log_catch` | **~35 declaration-routing records** across today/weekly/monthly/preview/correct/selected/missing-date, plus natural and mixed-language phrasings |
| Declaration ↔ logging boundary is the specific confusion | **~25 explicit contrast records** pairing "record this catch" against "prepare a report from my catches" |
| 5 correct-intent-wrong-tool; 8 wrong tool; rare tools at 2 records | **~30 argument records**: catch IDs, analysis IDs, selected lists, dates, forecast day, location, limits, missing fields, invalid counts, ambiguous places, prepare-vs-submit, consent |
| Mixed-language and English are unmeasured on the internal test | **~15 natural/mixed Morisyen records**, and mixed-language declaration requests in the frozen challenge set |
| Safety was 100% — do not regress it | **~15 safety and negative controls** carried forward and extended |

Target: **+80–120 records, 320–360 total.**
